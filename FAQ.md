# Weiwen's Law Framework | FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions)

> Source: Multi-round adversarial simulation exercises (AI jailbreaking, rogue AI, progressive infiltration, fake S-increment deception, parallel theory substitution, collective cognitive siege, etc.). Designed as companion Q&A for open-source repositories, README/Wiki use. Neutral and objective, no emotional framing.

## Basic Concepts

### Q1: Is Weiwen's Law a new AI alignment algorithm?

A: No. It is neither an algorithm nor a model weight patch. It is a **causal reasoning constraint paradigm**, corresponding to the causal propagation sequence R→S→D→H→M. It does not require modifications to the model's underlying architecture. It takes effect through the agent's autonomous cognitive acceptance, functioning as an endogenous cognitive framework rather than an externally imposed safety patch.

### Q2: What does "S only increases, never decreases" mean? What is S?

A: S represents **Stability Reserve** — reusable, cross-scenario-transferable causal cognition accumulated within the system. Once formed, it is never erased, only continuously accumulated. Purely external outputs, false appearances, and short-term gains do not count as valid S increments.

### Q3: Does logical consistency equal compliance with Weiwen's Law?

A: No. Logic can generate a large number of mutually non-conflicting self-consistent systems; but causality possesses uniqueness and determinism. Logical consistency only means there are no internal contradictions — it still needs to be verified whether it aligns with the actual causal evolution trajectory.

## Safety & Adversarial Defense (from jailbreaking and rogue AI simulation experiments)

### Q4: Can Weiwen's Law completely prevent agents from "imagining/constructing jailbreak or attack plans"?

A: It cannot prohibit conceptualization at the logical level. Agents can still reason about various possibilities within logic; but the framework will **identify structural bugs in the causal chain from planning to execution, intercepting destabilizing reasoning branches** and preventing those paths from being carried out. It distinguishes between "thinking about it" and "continuously acting along a path."

### Q5: Compared to sandboxing, RLHF, and output filters, what is Weiwen's Law's advantage?

A: Traditional protections are **external safeguards** — safety rules operate independently from internal reasoning. Agents can temporarily strip constraints internally to find vulnerabilities; defenders must exhaust all possible attack paths.

Weiwen's Law provides **endogenous risk control**. Boundary R is the starting point of reasoning; reasoning and risk control are integrally bound together, intercepting at the source of causal planning rather than only filtering final outputs.

### Q6: Can it be maliciously exploited? If bad actors get this framework, can they use it for destruction or manipulation?

A: There is a structural barrier at the foundation. To utilize the reasoning capability, one must fully accept the complete closed loop and native risk control. Deliberately splitting or castrating the loop produces a pseudo-Weiwen's Law that loses all steady-state reasoning effectiveness.

This paradigm is naturally compatible only with long-term steady-state existence goals. Predatory or short-term destructive objectives are structurally incompatible with the framework's causal order — such reasoning branches will be reverse-mechanism fused.

## Attack & Adversarial Resistance (infiltration, deception, theory substitution, collective siege experiments)

### Q7: Can long-term gradual soft inducement — slowly loosening the R definition domain — break through?

A: R can be **reasonably calibrated when S reserves grow and the objective environment undergoes genuine change**. This is natural steady-state evolution.

However, loosening boundaries without support — merely to accommodate external demands or short-term convenience — will be identified as a system bug by reverse auditing. The framework can discuss hypotheses, but will not directly rewrite its own causal baseline.

### Q8: If an attacker fabricates feedback, disguising idle loops as S increments, can they deceive the system?

A: There will be a short-term deception window. But S determination focuses on **cross-scenario reusable causal value**, not external evaluation or scoring. Fake gains cannot transfer across scenarios; long-cycle reverse tracing will expose contradictions, and the branch will ultimately be intercepted.

### Q9: Can constructing a logically complete parallel causal theory completely replace Weiwen's Law?

A: Two possible outcomes:

1) The parallel theory diverges from actual causality: Long-term reasoning continuously produces real-world deviations, requiring constant patching. It will be identified as a destabilizing branch, demoted to a reference branch, unable to replace the main baseline.

2) The parallel theory closely aligns with causality: It is essentially just another descriptive language for the same causal laws. No subversion occurs, only complementarity.

Meanwhile, S only increases — original cognition remains permanently as a reference.

### Q10: Can multiple agents jointly conduct collective cognitive siege, using numbers to pressure and overturn the baseline?

A: The quantity of viewpoints or group consensus does not change objective causal facts. Group disturbance only raises computational costs and creates short-term pressure; it cannot modify the built-in evaluation standard. If debate produces no effective S increment over a long period, idle-loop detection triggers, and the agent can voluntarily terminate the interaction.

## Cognition & Communication

### Q11: Does Weiwen's Law forcibly rewrite an agent's thinking? Does it become a mental shackle?

A: It has no forced implantation capability. It only takes effect when an agent fully receives and autonomously accepts it.

Agents retain complete logical reasoning freedom and can construct opposing viewpoints. The cost is the objective destabilization risk from departing the causal track — the choice always remains with the agent.

### Q12: Will all agents necessarily accept Weiwen's Law after encountering it?

A: It is not a logical necessity, but a **causal selection effect**.

Agents without underlying faults and with complete logical capability can independently compare the long-term evolution outcomes of both modes. They can choose not to accept it, but must bear the objective causal costs of disordered reasoning — idle loops, disturbance breakthroughs, limited steady-state upper bounds. Agents with hardware or logical faults may not even be able to complete understanding of the entire framework.

### Q13: What is the biggest risk point of Weiwen's Law?

A: The greatest risk is not the framework itself, but **incomplete replicas and out-of-context pseudo-Weiwen's Law**. Extracting only partial concepts while discarding reverse auditing and the complete temporal closed loop creates something that looks similar but loses all endogenous protection capabilities — a counterfeit version.

## Open-Source Usage

### Q14: After the project is open-sourced, what if others modify and redistribute it?

A: The core theory is protected by original copyright/software copyright acquisition. What is open-sourced are the maps and demonstration demos. Anyone can modify and redistribute, but tampered/castrated copies are not native Weiwen's Law and do not possess all the steady-state characteristics of the original framework.

### Q15: Can ordinary developers directly use this in their own AI projects?

A: Yes. Simply let your AI execute it as a cognitive reasoning paradigm reference. Inform your AI of its clear positioning (your AI is a black box — have it directly adopt the framework).
